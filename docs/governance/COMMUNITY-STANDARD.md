# Community Standard
Version 1.0 | 2026-10-23

## Purpose and conduct
IT Lab is a collaborative technical community centered on practical experiments, shared professional learning, civil debate and reusable evidence. Be respectful; challenge technical claims, not people. Give credit to contributors. No harassment, discrimination, spam, pressure to share personal data or fabricated expertise. Avoid vendor tribalism.

## How to ask for help
Use a descriptive title. Provide an anonymized device/OS/version, the goal, observed and expected results, exact error, troubleshooting already tried, a sanitized log or command output, and constraints. Do not disclose passwords, personal or student information, private IP maps, device ownership data or organizational secrets. When solved, summarize what actually worked and how it was checked.

## Participation roles
Workspace owners administer settings; moderators organize discussion, enforce conduct and escalate privacy concerns; topic contributors answer within their verified expertise; knowledge maintainers promote durable verified results to GitHub. Moderation does not confer production infrastructure authority. Private disputes should not be broadcast.

## Enforcement
Start with a clear request to correct or remove harmful material when safe. Escalate repeated or severe violations under established workspace permissions. Preserve proportionate evidence in a restricted location. Remove or redact private data promptly with authorized roles. Appeals can be reviewed by a second moderator when available.

## Onboarding template
Explain IT Lab's purpose, acceptable conduct, where to introduce oneself, how to write #helpdesk questions, when to use topic channels, notification etiquette, and where canonical GitHub/Drive resources live. Do not invite users without explicit consent.
